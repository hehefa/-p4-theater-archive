## Project statement

Iceberg was developed by eight makers and opened to an audience of fifteen inside a small office. Crowding, obstruction, silence and the difficulty of entering or leaving directly changed how the event could be seen.

The performance took place at 19:30 on 1 January 2022. Makers and spectators shared the same room without the stable distance of a proscenium stage.

A line through the work

## Two venue disruptions before Iceberg took shape

On December 7, 2021, He Fa described meeting Qu Wenhao, planning a production and forming a group again. After the Diaosutai venue stopped opening in June, a proposed ninth production initiated by Qu was halted. They began recruiting in a Wangjing office to develop Qu’s Iceberg. He Fa delegated most organizing work to Qu while occupied elsewhere; another venue problem arose in September. Iceberg’s development thus involved repeated changes of space, people and organization.

When restarting, the two decided to discuss shared values before forming North Wind Theatre. The article explicitly credits Qu with writing the nine-point declaration; He Fa endorsed it and joined the invitation. Its principles include respecting an initiator’s impulse, bringing personal concerns into roles, expressing views openly and addressing disagreement. The record therefore retains Qu’s authorship of the declaration and their joint initiation of the company.

## Iceberg

What does viewing become when there is no distant position?

### Begin with what happens in the work

Eight makers and fifteen spectators occupy an office of a little over ten square metres.

The public archive retains live material, smoke tests and post-performance discussion and criticism.

The space gives no one a frontal position from which everything can be safely surveyed. Another body blocks the view, and one’s own movement changes a neighbour’s sightline. Silence, crowding and difficulty moving become concrete materials. The intensity cannot be reproduced simply by enlarging a video. It comes from the bodily cost of looking in limited space and from the impossibility of imagining the spectator as an eye that leaves the event untouched.

Yet physical closeness proves neither a deep relation nor equal willingness to participate. Spatial facts should coexist with differing audience accounts rather than translating every discomfort into proof of artistic success. He Fa’s production role deserves a concrete description of how venue, schedule, light and documentation enabled collective work. The site can begin with the identifiable office and viewing positions, then let footage and criticism open that small space further.

### The limits of this reading

Still photographs and plans do not reproduce the live experience. The producer credit is not rewritten as sole director.
