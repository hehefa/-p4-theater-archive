# Begin with one Chinese character

Reading four groups of P4 documents through contemporary art

A single Chinese character turns “I would” into “I would not”; why do both versions still end with “Start again”?

Text comparison based on public editorial notes; not script facsimiles

A: seven answers of “I would”[Check the account](https://b.p4theater.cn/sources/public-last-rehearsal-seven-affirmative-answers)

A: seven answers of “I would not”[Check the account](https://b.p4theater.cn/sources/public-last-rehearsal-seven-negative-answers)

Shared final line, C: “Start again”

This is a comparison; the order in which the scripts were made is unresolved.

Compare seven instances of “I would” with seven instances of “I would not.” Both scripts for The Last Rehearsal end with “Start again.” The character 不 reverses the answer, while the final instruction remains. This small difference opens a way into four groups of documents: how words become actions, who gains a position from which to intervene, and how those actions are later made available to viewers.[[1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-1)

Three questions guide this reading of P4’s contemporary art practice: how an ending is made, how conditions of entry take shape, and how viewing is arranged. Script summaries, public articles, rule pages and editing-project accounts allow interpretation to return to specific materials.

## 1 How is an ending made?

The Last Rehearsal writes saving, loading and starting again into its script, making an ending an instruction that can be repeated. Jiarui’s post-performance recollection gives ending a physical arrangement: bodies are covered with a white cloth, the group moves backstage, and the cloth is left behind. At the exit, a question appears that he describes as outside the script. These materials invite a study of how a written ending encounters bodies, space and words spoken in the moment. The cloth first matters here through its work of covering bodies and enabling an exit.[[1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-1)[[2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-2)

The performance announcement distinguishes performing, recording, music and behind-the-scenes production. Another document, headed Script 3.0, places the director behind the camera and moves photographer D into the male lead’s position. This is a redistribution of functions within a written plan; it does not revise the documented responsibilities of real people.[[2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-2)

The script’s final line, a participant’s recollection and a limited visual account offer different records of ending. The order of the affirmative and negative versions, and the answers spoken in performance, remain unresolved. The visual summary supports lifting and covering with the cloth but cannot complete every action. Comparing these records helps readers see what each can establish.[[1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-1)[[2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-2)

## 2 Who can enter the work and propose a change?

Purple Action takes the form of a constructed fictional institution: organizations, a forum, deliberation and voting belong to the work. In a forum-style article, a demand for more individual speaking time develops into a proposal for eight candidates and eight deliberators. Replies raise the cost in time and the prospect of turning the encounter into a presentation session. The fictional planning group promises investigation and adjustment while defending discussion that is not restricted in advance. The disagreement concerns fuller statements, forms of expression and the basis for deciding. The documents preserve a proposal and a response; adoption is not established. Signatures within the work cannot be converted into claims about disputes between identified real people.[[3]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-3)

The 24-page introduction treats rule design as creative work and opens some institutions to participants. A ballot collage gives procedure a visible form through rounds, decisions and spaces for written reasons. The project descriptions attribute the initiation of its 2019 predecessor to Shen Bolun and the 2021 reworking to the Purple Action art group. He Fa has a place within the collective attribution; he does not stand for the group as a whole. Charters, ballots and forum formats make ways of organizing action available to read.[[4]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-4)

Killing Mother begins with another condition of entry: participants are asked to bring a piece of writing, whether original or borrowed, with no restriction on content. Choosing and bringing an existing text becomes part of preparation. How those texts were used, taken up or altered during an event still requires a specific record. Huang Meng’s published biography documents participation in co-creation. A public source note records Guo Qian’s credit on the archived original recruitment page. These establish different relationships without settling event roles.[[5]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-5)

## 3 Who arranges how later viewers will see?

3P places Performing, Peering and Processing together. Its manifesto also enters actual tracks in the 1.0 editing projects. Closing text in 1.0 distinguishes left-, middle- and right-side recording, directing and whole-event recording, allowing questions of authorship to be examined through particular tasks.[[6]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-6)

The 2.0 project preserves two different structures. One divides three image inputs into 33 segments while retaining long audio items; the other introduces three inputs at different times above a base image. Selection, layout and duration arrange viewing. Multiple cameras alone do not establish who holds the final editorial decision. The 1.0 credits cannot be assigned to 2.0 camera inputs or to people in images. Nor do 33 segments amount to 33 camera changes: presence inside an editing project does not establish visibility in a finished image.[[6]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents#citation-6)

## Continue through two groups of materials

“Seven answers and a white cloth” places textual variation beside a post-performance recollection and a visual summary. “Proposal, response and rules” places a demand for speaking time beside an institutional response and pages describing procedures. Both are online document-reading entries. Original public articles, the public PDF and editorial source notes retain their own labels, allowing readers to follow a word, a proposal or an arrangement back into the work.
