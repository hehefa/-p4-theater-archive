## Turning the ring into a theatre of judgment

Saturday Four-Hour Duel Club is a performance and game model within Purples. Six recruits entered as “fighters,” bringing their lived experiences while seeking audience support. The event on 23 October 2021 took place in a Beijing boxing gym, with a four-hour countdown and five eliminations. Performance, persuasion and audience votes determined who remained, rather than fighting skills.

The call required no professional performance experience and proposed several workshop-style rehearsals. The ring, countdown, voting marbles and scales made a mechanism of allocation visible: some people competed for attention while others occupied the position of spectator and judge. He Fa co-initiated Purples with Shen Bolun and Shao Yifan, and this work developed within that collaboration.

## Writing after the four hours

Four transcripts published in 2023 preserve Hours One to Four, keeping the host’s, fighters’ and spectators’ voices within the unfolding event. The call and record can be read together to examine how a rule was used through speech, questions, silence and voting. Original role labels are retained without assigning unverified real names to their lines.

## A later presentation proposal

Voting with marbles in the contemporary event materials and counting votes through drinks in the later Presentation Proposal belong to different versions. The proposal retains six fighters, four hours and five eliminations while setting out another arrangement of rules and scheduling. Read these proposed conditions alongside the record of the event on 23 October 2021.

## Saturday Four-Hour Duel Club

What changes when the audience can vote—and what stays?

### Begin with what happens in the work

Six “fighters” enter a boxing venue, seeking audience support through a four-hour countdown and five elimination rounds.

Marbles and weighing scales take part in deciding who stays; victory is not determined by fighting skill.

The ring borrows the space of competition and turns the contest toward securing support. Performance, lived experience, persuasion and audience judgment share a limited duration. This is not a simple advance from passive watching to active participation. Voting has consequences, but elimination restricts those consequences to staying or leaving. Spectators judge while fighters must keep seeking retention. The difference between these positions becomes materially visible in scales and a countdown.

The four-hour transcript published in 2023 adds a record of the rules being used. Readers can move from the invitation’s promises into live speech and examine what hosting, questioning, silence and persuasion did to the arrangement. Any discussion of the work’s politics should stay with these distributions rather than announcing democracy simply because a vote exists. Rules begin an event, and people within it reveal wishes and frustrations the rules have not settled.

### The limits of this reading

The performance year is distinguished from transcript publication; lines are not assigned real identities without a verified correspondence.
