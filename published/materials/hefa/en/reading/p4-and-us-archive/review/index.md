# Fa’s review: When Assessment Enters the Work

A work list, a statement about artistic identity and several camera positions bring the occasion of judgement inside the work.

## The work list identifies an assessment

A work list signed Fa He lists four pieces dated 28 April 2017, all labelled Documentary; one is described as another perspective. The end of the list identifies the assessment notice, preparatory statement and artist statement as important materials in the footage. This arrangement shows He Fa presenting the assessment and its documents as work, rather than merely keeping video as proof of an educational experience.

## The statement returns the question to the occasion of judgement

The associated statement does not begin by explaining an object’s materials and dimensions. It repeatedly asks how artistic identity is named, recognised and judged, connecting selection by a school, knowledge imparted by teachers and becoming an artist. The question returns to those assessing: judging work also involves the criteria by which the judge recognises artistic identity.

The statement also juxtaposes AI with moments when a choice cannot be determined. This is the position advanced by that text, not a factual claim here about AI’s capabilities. It places making in choices that established knowledge cannot fully resolve, making the assessment itself a situation worth observing.

[Read the associated statement](/en/work/fas-review#chapter-2) — Compare identity and indeterminate choices: the artist’s position, not the assessors’ replies.

## The cameras do not produce a tidy image of a defence

Existing samples from the source recordings show equipment, a cardboard box and a bottle in front of a low camera; seated speakers are not always fully visible. Another view is partly blocked by a large roll of paper and a tabletop. A short recording turns from people towards a text-covered wall, tables and equipment. Movement, obstruction and ordinary objects make this more than an abstract exchange of words: each position sees and misses different things.

The four files should not be treated as four independent events. They can initially be read as candidate views of the same assessment situation. Device clocks were not synchronised, however, and durations in the submitted work list differ from local source-file durations. Exact correspondence between views remains unestablished.

## What transformation does the evidence support?

A reading becomes possible: where an artwork would usually precede its assessment, Fa’s review uses selection and documentary arrangement to include being assessed within the work. He Fa is both the submitter and the subject under discussion; cameras make the occasion available to another act of watching. This is a concrete formal action without a need to assume successful institutional resistance.

Other positions must not be invented to complete the story. The statement preserves the artist’s questions; reliable evidence here does not reconstruct assessors’ replies sentence by sentence or establish the final judgement. Posture cannot be translated into displeasure, approval or resistance, and the artist’s questions are not automatically the assessors’ views.

## What a translation interface brings to the comparison

Fafalish operation records show letterforms, framing and machine-recognition output changing in relation to one another. Library photographs gather a computer, paper and onlookers around a table. What gets read depends on interface and operation. In the assessment, what counts as work depends on statements, viewing positions and a procedure of judgement. The comparison concerns how recognition takes place; it is an editorial reading, not established causation between successive works.
