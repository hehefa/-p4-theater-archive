WORK WORLD / OPEN FROM HERE

Can abandoning an image also be a way of completing it?

## Making, and deciding to stop

Untitled was made between 2012 and 2014. The artist calls it his most complex image and writes in the portfolio: “I kept saying no to her; in the end, I finally gave her up.” The statement locates the work in a particular process of making, rejecting and ultimately leaving an image as it stands.

## The density of the monochrome image

Against a dark ground, many small photographs open out into a landscape held within a rectangular boundary. Compared with the three colour variations of Shanshui, layering and repetition unfold here in a darker image. The listed 400 × 250 cm print gives those dense fragments room to be viewed at close range.

## Density and the decision to stop

Small grey-white images interrupt one another against a dark ground; adding detail does not make the whole easier to establish. Where Shanshui allows a return between mountain form and photographs, recognition here is harder to resolve. The artist’s account of abandoning the work can also be read as a decision to stop revising: a record of the method’s limits rather than a simple declaration of failure.

Editorial translation of the Chinese portfolio statement.

In the dense black-and-white image, fragments accumulate inside a containing rectangle. The time of making does not close neatly with a triumphant completion date. Abandonment preserves an unresolved relation between maker and outcome. The displayed image is not evidence that all its problems were solved. It is a version allowed to remain, bringing rejection, exhaustion and stopping into the work’s visible history.

The work is useful for considering later time-limited theatre, but it is not a prediction of that theatre. Retrospect reveals two different answers to the problem of ending. An endlessly revised image may never satisfy its maker; a public performance date puts the limit in a shared agreement. In one case the artist finally stops changing the work. In the other, a group must face a particular moment. Their juxtaposition asks how each work acquires its boundary.

### The limits of this reading

Abandonment is the portfolio’s wording, not evidence of an inferred personal event. The connection to later theatre is this reading’s proposal.
