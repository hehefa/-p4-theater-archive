## How the work took shape

Cocoon is a dance-theatre work concerned with prejudice. Writing on 29 October 2020, He Fa records that director Huang Yiyin agreed the schedule with him and Zhang Shaohua in September. Recruitment began in early October, continuous rehearsals on the sixteenth, and the presentation followed at the end of the month. A production of nearly twenty people brought performers, dancers, lighting, documentation, publicity and audiences into a shared period of preparation. November’s backstage interviews continued the account through individual participants’ experiences.

Huang explains in the interviews that an initial concern with LGBTQ experiences developed into a broader inquiry into prejudice across genders and individuals. He Fa’s writing also records fatigue, lateness, physical strain, judgement and mutual support during production. This page preserves the work as both a performance and an event of working together: how bodies are seen, and how collaboration alters participants’ understanding of themselves.

## He Fa’s writing at the time · 2020

“Theatre cannot happen only on the stage.” He Fa includes conversation, photography, argument and a passing glance among the conditions through which theatre happens. In the backstage interview, he stresses that differing roles do not prevent participants from expressing their views.

## Cocoon

Whose bodies and time make the event possible?

### Begin with what happens in the work

Rehearsal began on 16 October 2020, involving nearly twenty people; He Fa’s contemporary text describes the shared work.

Backstage interviews retain differing accounts of prejudice, bodies and collaboration from the director and participants.

This dance-theatre work cannot be understood only through attractive performance images. The contemporary text records repeated falls on a slippery stage, lateness, fatigue, injury, hunger and mutual judgments. These are not irrelevant matters outside creation. Whether a body can continue, and how members allocate time from their lives, directly determine what the evening can contain. Collective intensity has a specific process of production; final excitement cannot by itself establish a liberation shared by everyone.

He Fa stresses both equal respect and the necessity of continuing. Their coexistence has more intellectual weight than a frictionless account of co-creation: the ability to voice an opinion does not mean equal exposure to pressure. Performance, rehearsal schedule and backstage accounts should be read against one another. His importance here lies partly in organization and documentation. Assigning the entire production to him would conceal the collaborative structure through which it became possible.

### The limits of this reading

Participant accounts remain individual accounts; the process is not presented as a verified therapeutic or collective psychological effect.

Based on He Fa’s signed text of 29 October 2020 and the November performance report and backstage interviews.
