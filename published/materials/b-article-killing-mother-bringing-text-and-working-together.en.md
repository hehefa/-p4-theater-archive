PROJECT READING · PUBLIC EVIDENCE

# Killing Mother: after bringing a text, how do we work together?

From an invitation to bring writing to a published account of Huang Meng’s participation, this reading traces how participation enters the archive and asks how a text becomes shared work.

[Project reading: Begin with one Chinese character](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/reading-p4-documents)

Editorial reading of a public invitation and an institutional participation account; the use of texts within the event remains unverified · reading-1 · 2026-10-08

## No original script is required at the door

The call for Killing Mother gives participants a concrete preparation task: bring a piece of writing to the venue. It may be original or borrowed, with no restriction on content. Describing the activity as a theatrical “duel exercise,” the announcement schedules an afternoon gathering in Wangjing, Beijing, on 4 June 2023.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-s1)

Writing an original script is therefore not a condition of entry. Someone can begin by choosing and bringing an existing text, then encounter the work of the group. Selection itself can be read as a starting point for creation. This is an interpretation of the invitation; the available documents do not establish what any participant actually brought.

## From an advertised place to a named participant

The May invitation offers eight places. A later public introduction, P4’s Final Chapter, published on 25 August, states in Huang Meng’s biography that she had participated in co-creating the Killing Mother theatre salon. The saved original publication HTML also links that statement back to the recruitment article.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-s1)[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-s2)

The archive gains something specific: alongside an intended number of participants, there is now a publicly attributed participant in co-creation. This is an institutional account of someone’s experience, not independent third-party testimony. It supports a documented relationship between Huang Meng, co-creation and Killing Mother. It does not establish her attendance at a particular 4 June session, allocate her tasks, or identify her in a recording.[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-s2)

## A page credit and a production role are different records

The original recruitment page carries Guo Qian’s name as its author credit. That helps trace the publication of the text. It does not assign directing, hosting or organizing duties at the event. This reading preserves the page credit without turning it into a claim that Guo Qian directed the salon.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-s1)

An author credit and a retrospective statement of co-creation work at different levels. One connects a person to a text; the other connects a person to a practice. Specific working records would still be needed to establish who proposed a task, organized responses or changed an arrangement. The appearance of names cannot supply those missing steps.

## One person can belong to several works

P4’s Final Chapter also links a text for Huang Meng titled “…Then You Became a Little Girl Again…”. A note beneath the list identifies the linked texts as writing prepared for The Last Rehearsal performance. The same introduction credits Huang Meng as a performer in that work.[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-s2)

These records allow a person’s creative history to be read across projects. They do not establish that the later article was the text she brought to Killing Mother. Nor can a performing credit for The Last Rehearsal be transferred into a specific responsibility in Killing Mother.[[R1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-r1) Connections between people and works remain useful when the circumstances of each text and each role stay distinguishable.

## How does a brought text become shared work?

Two supported steps can now be placed together: the invitation asks participants to bring writing, and a later public account names one participant in co-creation. What happens to the writing within the event remains undocumented here.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-s1)[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/killing-mother-bringing-text-and-working-together#ref-s2)

Further research can begin with concrete actions: who selects a passage, how is it voiced, how do others respond, and does the expression change through that encounter? These are questions proposed by this essay, not a reconstructed sequence. Participant texts, working notes or checked event records tied to a specific session would be needed to connect those steps.

The earlier Three Kinds of Time essay examines differences between planned, camera-recorded and published dates. This essay adds an attributed participation record while retaining those timing questions. A post-event reflection whose author, date and specific session remain unverified is not used here to narrate the event. No new visual identification, listening check or raw-to-edit timing map is claimed.
