## Project statement

Completed in 2012, Mountain Beyond Mountains was He Fa’s graduation photography work. Photographing, dividing and recomposing organize many partial images into a landscape; it forms a whole at a distance and separates into time, viewpoint and fragment up close.

Mountain Beyond Mountains — Red and White entered the CAFA Art Museum collection. The work also established a line He Fa would return to: looking organizes space, bodies and relations.

ORIGINAL PROCESS ARCHIVE · AN INQUIRY INTO SHANSHUI

## From photographs to brushstrokes: eight stages in the graduation defense

The original graduation defense, titled An Inquiry into Shanshui, records method studies, two approaches to arrangement and the final works. The eight illustrated stages below follow its order. Artist statements are translated from Chinese; the accompanying reading notes are editorial.

Since 2008, I have been trying to use photography to express the Chinese landscape in my mind. In the Shanshui series, I try to dissolve the photographs’ original content and information, turning them into “brushstrokes” in order to construct an ideal space.

He Fa, original graduation defense, p. 2. Translated from Chinese.

PROCESS 01 / 08

## Movement through image shifts

Shifting the images brings changes in movement.

Artist statement · defense p. 4 · translated from Chinese

The initial experiment concerns the sense of movement in an image. The photograph ceases to act as a fixed window: shifts disturb its edges and details, changing the movement of the composition.

An Inquiry into Shanshui, graduation defense, p. 4. Full composition of the embedded image retained.

PROCESS 02 / 08

## Scaling and displacement

Repeated scaling and displacement of the images produce an illusion between form and formlessness.

Artist statement · defense p. 5 · translated from Chinese

Scaling changes the relative size of the photographs; displacement lets a scene recur in different positions. The artist describes the resulting state as an illusion between form and formlessness: the landscape remains perceptible, but no single camera position accounts for it.

An Inquiry into Shanshui, graduation defense, p. 5. Full composition of the embedded image retained.

PROCESS 03 / 08

## 1,025 photographs and the trace of walking

I used 1,025 landscape photographs taken at Siguniang Mountain in Sichuan as material for experiments in forms of combination.

Because they were taken from different locations, each photograph has a different perspective. Combined, they move from the single-point perspective of conventional photography to a thoroughly dispersed perspective. This composition records the trace of my walking and my memory of the whole mountain.

Artist statement · defense p. 7 · translated from Chinese

The defense presentation specifies the number of source photographs and their location for this experiment. The figure 1,025 refers to this group from Siguniang Mountain; it does not establish the photograph count of every work in the series. The differences between camera positions matter: the route of looking becomes part of the composition.

An Inquiry into Shanshui, graduation defense, p. 7. Full composition of the embedded image retained.

PROCESS 04 / 08

## First arrangement: a grid organized by color

The first method is an uncontrolled grid arrangement. I place the source photographs in the grid according to their color relationships, seeking only a faint distinction between ground, mountains and sky.

Artist statement · defense p. 8 · translated from Chinese

The grid first provides a framework for holding photographs. Color relationships then form broad spatial areas. A local image does not need to describe an identifiable place; the relation of ground, mountains and sky only needs to emerge faintly.

An Inquiry into Shanshui, graduation defense, p. 8. Full composition of the embedded image retained.

PROCESS 05 / 08

## Working the grid: layers, shifts and opacity

I overlay and displace the photographs within each grid cell and change their opacity, allowing their content to recede. We seem to see ground, mountains and sky, yet also seem to see nothing.

Perhaps it is by dissolving representational forms in this way that the viewer’s inner feelings can be awakened.

Artist statement · defense p. 9 · translated from Chinese

At this stage, the recognizability of each photograph changes. Layering and opacity let specific scenes recede while the larger mountain form remains intermittent. The original statement tentatively links this dissolution to the viewer’s feelings.

An Inquiry into Shanshui, graduation defense, p. 9. Full composition of the embedded image retained.

PROCESS 06 / 08

## Second arrangement: composing space subjectively

The second method is a freer arrangement under subjective control. It can better express my understanding of space.

Artist statement · defense p. 10 · translated from Chinese

A second route gives the artist more direct control over the positions and relationships of the photographs. The presentation places it alongside the grid method: one builds an overall field through cells; the other seeks space through subjective arrangement.

An Inquiry into Shanshui, graduation defense, p. 10. Full composition of the embedded image retained.

PROCESS 07 / 08

## Reworking photographs into a shared atmosphere

I then rework each photograph again, seeking an atmosphere in which they permeate one another.

Artist statement · defense p. 11 · translated from Chinese

After arranging the images, the artist continues to work on them individually so that different views, scales and levels of transparency act together. Arrangement is an intermediate stage; the way images permeate one another is also part of the making.

An Inquiry into Shanshui, graduation defense, p. 11. Full composition of the embedded image retained.

PROCESS 08 / 08

## Combining both arrangements

Finally, I tried overlaying the results of the two methods, creating a state of seeing mountains as mountains, then not as mountains, then as mountains again. Although the forms in each individual photograph are dissolved, the mountain’s form becomes more pronounced. This form is my experience in the mountains, my understanding and interpretation of them.

Artist statement · defense p. 12 · translated from Chinese

The final step overlays the results of both routes. Forms within individual photographs recede, while the mountain as a whole becomes more pronounced. The artist identifies this composite with experience, memory and interpretation in the mountains, rather than the reproduction of a fixed viewpoint.

An Inquiry into Shanshui, graduation defense, p. 12. Full composition of the embedded image retained.

## Another early portfolio record

This early portfolio presents Shanshui through a horizontal landscape. Distant mountains, nearer trees and sky are joined from many photographs. Differences in brightness and scale are not completely smoothed away. The image retains both the broad shape of a landscape and traces of its construction; peaks, foliage and blocks of colour become visible at different distances.

In a preserved statement, the artist begins with the act of sorting photographs. He describes assembling more than a thousand photographs from a trip to Huangshan and relating this process to a moving viewpoint in landscape painting. The portfolios record different dimensions and media for Shanshui. Those differences are retained here as distinct records, rather than treated as a single exhibition specification.

Pages 14–15 of the 38-page portfolio record a 2400 × 1250 mm light box. Page 12 of the 13-page portfolio records 520 × 520 mm and mixed media. Both give January 2012. These are version records for Shanshui, not evidence of separate works.

## The 2012 thesis: from modes of looking to photographic brushstrokes

Read alongside the graduation presentation’s process images, the 2012 thesis Exploring Shanshui Photography places the work between three modes of looking: shifting viewpoints in Chinese landscape painting, mobile viewpoints in David Hockney’s photocollages, and Cézanne’s late construction of a whole through patches of colour. He Fa translates these readings into his own method, treating photographs taken along a walk as elements of differing texture, light, darkness and colour.

Chapter Five again records an experiment using 1,025 photographs taken at Siguniang Mountain in Sichuan, superimposing gridded and subjective arrangements. The intended viewing moves between the whole and the detail: “The viewer experiences what I experienced, and feels what I felt.”

## Beginning with photographs of Huangshan

In two early statements, He Fa recalls reassembling more than a thousand photographs taken during a visit to Huangshan while sorting his pictures. He noticed a resemblance between photographic assembly and a view that changes as one moves through a landscape. Finding that an imitation of this form was insufficient, he returned to nature and brought the experience of walking back into his work.

This recollection records an early experiment with Huangshan photographs. The graduation presentation separately documents an assembly experiment using 1,025 photographs of Mount Siguniang. The two sources retain the distinct locations and image counts associated with the initial attempt and the later methodological experiment.

Source documents: 作品自述（何发目录）; 作品阐释（照片目录）.

## From sketches to exhibition

The graduation plan divides the work into sorting existing landscape photographs, reading about painting, drawing and revising sketches, producing the green, white and red works, and then adjusting the images and making lightboxes. February through April are assigned to the three images, May to revisions and lightbox production, and June to exhibition. This planned rhythm shows image experiments, physical presentation and thesis discussion developing together.

Source documents: 本科生毕业设计指导书——何发.

## Taking one photograph apart

The midterm presentation begins with a photograph of rocks and trees. It divides the image, shuffles the parts and turns recognizable scenery into elements of different light, colour and texture. A subjective, painterly arrangement reassembles them; layering and displacement then change their boundaries. Vertical and horizontal shaking and changes of scale follow, before the experiment expands to a mountain assembled from 1,025 photographs.

Source documents: 山水之求索——中期汇报, pp. 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12.

## Three ways of organizing attention

Green organizes depth through flanking mountains and a pale valley. In White, tree trunks and a path into the snow establish direction. Red sets ochre peaks against a blue-grey sky. Rectangular fragments repeatedly cross these spatial boundaries: just as the eye reads an inhabitable landscape, blocks of colour and seams return it to the image surface. The three works offer different rhythms of looking, beyond variations in palette.

CLOSE READING / 2026 CURATORIAL INTERPRETATION

## Shanshui

Can a mountain hold many acts of looking at once?

### Begin with what happens in the work

The series uses photography, digital montage and light boxes; the records include red, green and white works.

The early thesis outline connects the experience of climbing, the limitations of a single viewpoint and the study of montage.

At a distance a mountain takes shape; nearby it breaks into separate acts of framing. The viewer’s movement is therefore more than an optional way to appreciate scale. It is a temporal mechanism: stepping back compresses differences into an image, while moving closer releases them again. Each photograph records something it once faced, but no individual photograph witnessed the final mountain. The whole is made and should not be mistaken for panoramic proof of a particular site.

The important issue is not an abundance of information but its redistribution through a medium. Climbing involves direction, resistance and duration; the image makes these available on one surface. Montage compensates for the single viewpoint while introducing a different loss. A presentation should therefore let viewers see the complete work, inspect details and return to the whole. An automatic tour can suggest one reading, but should not prescribe its only sequence.

### The limits of this reading

Image counts and output versions need separate verification. Digital viewing does not reproduce the scale or transmitted light of the original light boxes.

Based on the public work page, portfolio and early landscape-photography thesis materials. Later theatre theories are not treated as the original motivation of the 2012 work.
