03 / IMAGE AND DURATION

## The body leaves. Time remains.

Sizeng, Zengcheng and Yucen enter the same forest. Doors, paper, glass and water retain their passages without assembling them into one shared memory. After someone leaves, the space continues; a body in another image may still be waiting.

The work assigns no fixed past, present or future to its three figures, and day and night do not divide reality from dreaming. Long takes retain the duration of staying and leaving. Layers cross cuts: the next passage has begun before the previous one has left.

Body, paper and wall: contact leaves a trace, but the trace does not yield a complete story.

Two images occupy one plane: a layer continues across the cut.

Here, the Odyssey first means recognition after return, rather than a retold adventure. Does arrival let someone recognize home? If the place remains, does it recognize the returning person? The title keeps this unverifiable experience open, without making one ending answer for every body.

04 / A POSITION FOR THE VIEWER

## Look toward one side and temporarily lose the other.

The following retains the two-screen and sound structure of the earlier Corridor V4 edition. It is presented separately from the live documentation above; the diagram does not reconstruct the recorded installation.

Each image plane loops for approximately one hour. The two sides form neither a panorama nor a complete answer. While the viewer faces one side, the other continues. Turning, approaching and missing become conditions of seeing.

Six spatial sound channels share a playhead and clock, looping independently over 37 minutes 17 seconds. Each wall also has its own sound. The unequal sound and image cycles change their encounters. A and B have equal image durations and do not drift once their start is fixed. These six custom sound positions are not cinema 5.1.

The single-screen web editions contain that wall’s picture and wall sound only, without the six-point spatial layer. Sound captions describe documented paper, cloth, water, rain and ambience. “Wall-sound pause” does not mean the whole installation is silent.

05 / TEXT AND LIVE BODY

## Can a memory leave the body it came from?

The thirteen-part monologue begins with coming back, yet cannot settle who has returned. Money, interest, calculation, consent, screens and bodies mingle; a pronoun changes position before its sentence ends. Chinese homophones and the changing meanings of 还 turn language itself into a site of recognition.

He Fa reads the thirteen-part text in the live performance. He Yu raises the candle, pauses and turns; her movement and He Fa’s voice keep their own rhythms. At the end, they sit facing each other. The relation between body and voice extends beyond a sentence-by-sentence correspondence.

The Chinese preserves the original wording, line breaks and spacing. The English is an editorial translation for this edition; reading both keeps visible the sound relations that cannot fully translate.

06 / CONTINUITIES

## Carry the question forward, not the forest.

Translation, accent and transcription prevent language from returning unchanged. This monologue places that displacement inside a single speaker.

Connected seats and screens give viewing a bodily position. The two-screen corridor asks what one gives up seeing in order to see.

The person filming is also inside the relationship; the camera offers no final neutral position. Here, sound, image and body retain accounts that do not fully agree.

These are editorial connections proposed in this presentation, not claims that the works belong to one event.
