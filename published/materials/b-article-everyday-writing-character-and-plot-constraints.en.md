# Out of the Everyday / Thirty-three Lives: who enters the story, what drives a life

From character and plot rules to four story slots for each of eight author positions and a shared opening, examine task allocation and differing historical settings in the table and invitation. Also compare how two invitations present the same proposal.

[Project reading: Out of the Everyday: from a shared opening to the next writing task](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide)

Public proposal and editorial account of an unpublished allocation sheet; use, assignment acceptance and delivery remain unverified · reading-3 · 2026-10-10

## Who can count as an ordinary person here?

The recruitment article for Out of the Everyday, published on 6 April 2023, includes a proposal for the Thirty-three Lives series signed by 余裕雨木 (Yu Yu Yumu). It invites collective work on fictional lives of ordinary people, with a specific condition: the characters must be able to record their own lives.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s1) That condition shapes the selection. The text names owner-cultivators, blacksmiths, monks, scholars and merchants, and explicitly observes that its main characters would mostly occupy mainstream social structures and have somewhat above-average social standing. This is the proposal author's account of a bias in the premise, not a historical statistical finding by this library. These characters cannot simply stand for every ordinary person's life.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s1)

## Unusual stories may be told without governing the plot

The proposal draws a second boundary. Strange or extraordinary stories may occur in characters' conversations, but should not determine the direction of the fictional world. Work, everyday life and taxation are offered as threads for organising a life story.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s1) This page reads those choices as a writing arrangement; it does not treat the recruitment text's broad historical statements as independently verified historical findings. The distinction separates what a character hears from what actually drives a life. It gives collaborators a concrete question: a rumour may enter dialogue, but can it also cause a decisive change in the story? The proposal supplies a rule for considering that question. This is an editorial reading of a public premise, not evidence that later participants accepted or followed it.

## Eight author positions become thirty-two individual tasks

The newly checked Eight-author Allocation workbook makes the division of work more concrete. Its allocation sheet uses merged cells to group four rows under each of eight author positions: 32 individual story slots. Row 34 separately proposes a jointly written opening, The False Indra. This is a work-allocation design, not 33 completed works.[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s2)

The entries offer different amounts of material. Some invite a starting point in the writer’s own experience; others specify a period or a character; some include a plot outline and a provisional title. Collaborators are not being offered identical blank prompts. This reading interprets shared writing as also involving the distribution of tasks at different stages of development. Outlines and provisional titles are not delivery records.

Most author fields remain background placeholders rather than a list of eight attendees. The second author position contains the name Zhang Xuejie (张雪杰). That establishes an entry in the table, not acceptance of an assignment, attendance or completed stories. It does not establish an identity link with Yu Yu Yumu. Fictional characters’ lives are not treated as participants’ biographies.[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s2)

## The opening has a place, but its historical setting differs

The joint-writing row names a scholar Feng, a blacksmith Zhang and a farmer Zheng, and proposes The False Indra as the series opening. It distinguishes a shared beginning from the individual story groups. The workbook establishes that this task was written down; it does not establish how the opening was started or completed in a session.[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s2)

That row labels its period “before Qin and Han”, with an adjacent note referring to Qin Shi Huang. The public invitation of 6 April 2023 instead places the series opening in the reign of Emperor Wu of Han. This is a directly checkable difference between documents.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s1)[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s2) Without dated revision records or evidence of use, neither can be established as a revision of the other. Unchecked machine transcripts cannot settle a final version.

## Rules and allocation still need records of acceptance and delivery

Character selection and narrative causality set conditions for shared writing; the workbook divides tasks into individual story groups and a joint opening. More specific checks now become possible: did writers use the same allocation sheet? Which rules entered their stories? Did the opening’s period or characters change in signed, dated drafts?[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s1)[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s2)

The workbook supplies a missing layer of task design. It does not supply completed assignment records, submissions or finished drafts. File properties and backup-directory dates are not treated as dates of revision or activity. Only an editorial account of ordinary project work is published here; the complete workbook is not attached.

## Two invitations: bringing the writing proposal to readers

The recruitment articles of 6 and 8 April 2023 place the same writing proposal in different reading sequences. The first introduces a long-term collaboration, presents the five-part proposal signed by Yu Yu Yumu, then invites people to join Out of the Everyday. The second moves the recruitment passage ahead of those five parts: readers encounter the invitation before the account of what to write, why to write it and how to choose characters.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s1)[[S3]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s3)

The 8 April recruitment passage explicitly seeks eight creators while retaining an invitation open to all ages and professional backgrounds. Eight was not a newly introduced number: the 6 April introduction already called for eight creators. The visible text of the five parts, from “01 Proposal” to “05 About Myself”, is identical across the two pages. The change concerns the placement and wording of the invitation, rather than a rewrite of those five parts.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s1)[[S3]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-s3)

This makes the presentation of writing rules to potential collaborators a traceable part of the work. A full proposal can precede the invitation, or readers can be invited in before the shared task is explained. The pages document these different invitations; they do not establish how many people signed up, who accepted the allocation-sheet tasks or what manuscripts were subsequently delivered.

## Comparing tasks across projects without claiming influence

The existing BODY study considers how abstract relationships become playable for performers; the forum-theatre study examines how a second run gives spectators a place to act.[[R1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-r1)[[R2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-r2) These offer ways to compare how tasks are proposed, without combining them into a proven shared method.

The fixed edition of How Shared Work Leaves a History separates proposals, responses, actions and outcomes.[[R3]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-writing-character-and-plot-constraints#ref-r3) Evidence for this project now extends from a published writing premise to a directly readable allocation design, while implementation remains unverified. Credit for the signed premise stays with Yu Yu Yumu. The workbook’s compiler and actual assignees are not inferred, and circulation through P4 does not make the design He Fa’s independent work.
