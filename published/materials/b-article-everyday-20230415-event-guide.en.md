# Out of the Everyday: from a shared opening to the next writing task

Proposals, improvisation and responses across eight recordings: whether shared creation needs agreement, how historical rules constrain characters and what can be written next.

## From a shared opening to a story of one's own

These Out of the Everyday / Thirty-three Lives materials show how a long-term writing proposal enters shared work: introductions and intentions lead to a proposed historical scene; an improvisation is followed by discussion of what it did and did not accomplish; the next step is reduced to a short story from each willing contributor. This sequence connects themes across eight published recordings and their machine transcripts. It does not reconstruct the whole gathering without gaps.[[V1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v1)[[V2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v2)[[V3]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v3)[[V4]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v4)[[V5]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v5)[[V6]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v6)[[V7]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v7)[[V8]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v8)

## What the proposal set out

The 6 April invitation carries a proposal signed by 余裕雨木 (Yu Yu Yumu). It organises a long historical span through fictional lives of ordinary people able to record their experiences. Extraordinary stories may be discussed, but should not determine the main plot. The proposal itself acknowledges that these characters tend towards mainstream social structures; they cannot stand for everyone.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-s1)

The 8 April invitation moves recruitment ahead of the same five-part proposal. Eight creators had already appeared in the 6 April introduction. A separate allocation sheet assigns four story positions to each of eight author positions, plus a shared opening. Its compilation and use dates are unconfirmed, so it cannot be placed in a certain chain of revisions before or after the session.[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-s2)[[S3]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-s3) These documents provide comparisons, not proof of which sheet appears or was used on site.

## Does working together require thinking alike?

The opening recording includes notice of recording and online publication, followed by responses about long-term collaboration, differences in values and authorship. One expressed position allows participation in the process without a credit as an author; others invite people willing to take that role to come forward. These responses concern authorship of proposed work. They do not constitute a settled contributor list, and the opening notice alone does not establish every person's full permission status.[[V1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v1)

Another recording develops the reasons for the shared-writing proposal and for joining the activity. Some remarks approach history through ordinary lives; another perspective questions the preference for unusual subjects in familiar forms of documentation. Long-term acquaintance, interest in writing and encounters between viewpoints therefore offer different reasons to participate.[[V2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v2) The material does not consolidate them into an agreed motivation. Literary and historical examples remain participants' arguments, not historical findings endorsed by this guide.

## Turning cooperation into an action that can begin

Discussion of cooperation proposes few prior rules alongside protective boundaries, while recognising that trust cannot simply be stipulated. This connects with a practical suggestion: make an opening together, become acquainted through the work, then divide the writing. The discussion begins to specify a scholar, a blacksmith, a farmer and a device for passing a record to later people.[[V3]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v3)

The scale of work changes here. A ten-year aspiration becomes a scene that can be tried now. This is an observable way of organising a task, not proof of an effective long-term institution. A reference to an allocation sheet in the machine transcript also does not establish that the document mentioned is the particular workbook summarised by this library.[[S2]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-s2)[[V3]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v3)

## Improvisation: one meal, different possible stories

The recording around floor mats preserves participants standing, sitting or reclining, gathering and responding. Its machine transcript suggests fictional situations involving metalwork, visitors, preparing a meal, drinking and family relationships. Characters supply one another with backgrounds; corrections about period-specific terms enter the scene as well.[[V4]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v4) These fictional relationships do not describe participants' families or biographies.

After the scene closes, a specific difficulty emerges in discussion: an opening intended to hand the narrative on to later people has not reached that intended point. Other explanations focus on why an apprentice seeks new opportunities, or how a visitor and family relationships were understood. The same scene can thus be read as an unfinished transmission task or as the emergence of motives that could develop further.[[V5]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v5) These readings do not establish stable opposing camps or control over a final version.

## Must there be one agreed opening?

The discussion asks whether shared creation requires collective agreement. Two distinguishable proposals appear in the machine text: use performance to work towards a shared opening, or keep individual openings and try them separately. There is no documented result establishing unanimous acceptance of either.[[V5]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v5)

A further difference concerns narrative boundaries: whether extreme events may drive the main plot, and how far ordinary lives must be constrained by the conditions of a particular period. The published proposal's limits on extraordinary stories offer a useful comparison, but do not identify the signed author as the speaker of particular recorded statements.[[S1]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-s1)[[V5]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v5)[[V6]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v6) This guide retains the tension between a creative rule and its responses without reproducing unverified harmful anecdotes or turning disagreement into a judgment of character.

Discussion then proposes historical reading, individually written openings, online exchange and a later shared trial. One interpretation compares the improviser's inability to control every development with the limits experienced by a character in a social setting.[[V6]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v6) That is a reading offered in the material, not evidence that rehearsal has verified a historical theory. Historical research and fictional choices remain different kinds of work.

## A short story for the following week

Later discussion reduces the next step to a short story from each contributor for the following week. Publication through a public account, reader responses and discussion are proposed as ways to sustain the work. Questions remain about how different approaches can coexist, how periods should be allocated and whether contemporary experience can provide a starting point.[[V7]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v7)

Choosing a period before writing is not the only route proposed. Another response permits writing a story first and matching it to a period later.[[V7]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v7) This is a concrete example of a task being adjusted. Yet spoken claims and requests to write names do not supply a verified named allocation record. This guide does not reconstruct a roster from machine recognition or turn next week's target into delivered work.

Closing discussion continues to address willingness to participate, ideas already taking shape and remote submissions. It also touches on permission to share a different performance recording and a possible future screening collaboration. Those exchanges show discussion reaching into other work; they do not establish granted permission or implemented cooperation.[[V8]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v8) The supported endpoint is a proposed next step. Completed drafts, actual publication and a subsequent meeting still require independent evidence.

## Returning to the recordings and continuing the inquiry

The eight viewing entries below each bind to a specific YouTube edition and link back to their source notes. This release adds no precise chapters. Machine-transcript chunks are not listening-verified topic boundaries, and source and platform timelines have not been calibrated frame by frame. In particular, Historical Characters and a Shared Opening runs 19:45.985 locally but is labelled 19:47 by the platform; the approximately 1.015-second difference has no established cause.[[V6]](https://b.p4theater.cn/en/research/everyday-20230415-event-guide#ref-v6) The guide therefore connects passages to specific recordings without inventing second-level links.

Useful next evidence includes an independent event-date record; confirmed named roles; listening-checked chapters tied to each public access edition; and dated allocations, drafts, publications and further rehearsals. Meanwhile, three readings remain possible: an unfinished shared opening; a rehearsal that exposes different character intentions; and a negotiation that turns a distant ambition into a next writing task. They attend to different outcomes and can coexist. None replaces evidence of what happened afterwards.
