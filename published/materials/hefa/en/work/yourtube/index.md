YourTube is He Fa’s 2018 MFA thesis work. It places images carrying traces of online remixing and danmaku comments within an installation of multiple screens, exposed cables, coloured tubes and connected stools. The work brings three questions together: how images direct attention, how other people’s comments shape what we see, and whether sitting together creates a relationship.

YOURTUBE / AN OVERVIEW

## Connections are in place. Relationships remain to be made.

The equipment is connected, the seats are linked, and comments already inhabit the images. Do these connections also give viewers the ability to understand one another, speak for themselves and change the arrangement? This question guides the reading below.

### Screens arrange attention

Images occupy different heights and orientations, but together they retain a shared front. Viewers can choose where to look without being able to see every image equally clearly.

### Seats arrange proximity

Six stools are joined into a ring by tubes. Neighbours have been assigned before anyone sits down. Proximity alone guarantees neither a shared direction nor mutual understanding.

### Comments bring earlier viewers into the image

Comments, jokes and interpretations are already on screen. We encounter a body together with traces of how others have watched it.

CONNECTION BEFORE RELATIONSHIP / READING THE WORK

## Objects, images and the viewer’s position

This reading draws on interpretations supplied by He Fa and a September 2026 research draft. It begins with visible features of the installation and develops their implications. These later interpretations are distinct from reports of what audiences experienced in 2018.

### 01　A front of screens, a ring of seats

The installation photographs show six CRT televisions along the floor and five flat-panel displays extending up the wall. Frames, volume, orientation and height keep the images distinct. Looking involves adjusting between upward, level and downward lines of sight.

Although dispersed, the screens retain a shared front. They offer several objects of attention within an arrangement selected by the artist. Choosing where to look and deciding what is available to look at are different powers.

Six backless stools, joined by tubes, form a roughly circular configuration. The ring establishes who sits beside whom without prescribing a single direction for every body. Its geometric centre lies inside the ring; the screens attracting attention stand outside it. The structure gathering people and the object gathering attention do not coincide.

One structure organises where to look. Another organises whom to sit beside.

Installation photograph 01. Two ways of organising an audience occupy the same space.

### 02　Connection precedes the audience

The stools are more than neighbouring objects. Tubes pass through openings in their sides, making separate everyday items parts of a connected structure. Each stool retains its own position while belonging to the larger configuration.

Before anyone sits down, proximity has already been arranged. Whether that arrangement becomes conversation, intimacy or understanding depends on how people use it. The photographs do not establish that seats could not be moved or that visitors were required to sit.

Black cables and coloured tubes should also be distinguished. The cables belong to the equipment’s wiring; the tubes shape the space and connect the seats. Visible wiring does not disclose who selected the images or who can change what is played.

Objects have been connected. Relationships between people have not thereby been completed.

Installation photograph 04. A material connection does not by itself prove community, solidarity or coercion.

### 03　How images are renamed and reused

According to the account of the work’s sources recorded in the supplied materials, YourTube engages with male homoerotic imagery and its transformation in online “philosophy” culture. In this context, “philosophy” is a community label associated with remixed male-body imagery, altered sound, phonetic reinterpretation and shared jokes; it does not refer to the academic discipline.

Translation here includes more than language. Editing changes the duration of an action; phonetic reinterpretation assigns new meanings to sounds; rhythmic repetition makes fragments reusable; comments supply further social interpretations. A body’s appeal may persist while the ways of approaching and discussing it change.

Body and performance Actions, sounds and their earlier context.

Editing and reinterpretation Extraction, repetition, sound alteration and new meanings.

Comments and shared readings Jokes, recognition cues and expectations enter the image.

Watching in the exhibition These traces encounter physical displays and viewing positions.

This is an analytical route, not a verified history of every clip. Titles, dates, production locations, remix versions and their assignments to screens require original playback files and production records.

### 04　Other people’s watching becomes part of what I see

On relevant danmaku interfaces, comments posted on different dates can be attached to the same position in a video. A later viewer encounters them when playback reaches that point. The research draft calls this “co-presence across different times” .

Comments can direct attention, mark moments of expectation and suggest recognisable responses. The viewer encounters an image already interpreted by others. “The prior socialisation of viewing” names this condition: other ways of watching are present before one’s own judgement has formed.

This does not mean everyone accepts the same reading. A familiar joke may welcome one viewer and confuse another. Comments can be agreed with, misunderstood, rejected or reinterpreted.

Danmaku preserves reactions and makes earlier reactions conditions of later viewing.

Installation photograph 08. The photograph shows layers of images and writing, but cannot establish every comment’s meaning or the mechanism for live input.

### 05　Why desire may appear as a joke

Within the joking frame of “philosophy” culture, someone may admire a body or repeatedly watch an action while describing that attention as a meme, technical appreciation or a joke. The draft calls this “retractable expressions of desire” : a feeling can remain unnamed without immediately becoming an identity declaration.

Such ambiguity can protect exploration, but it may also become a constraint. Being free to joke differs from being accepted only while joking. The question is whether a person who expresses sincere admiration, intimacy or disagreement can still receive a serious response.

The repeated visibility of male homoerotic bodies does not automatically establish respect for the people to whom those bodies belong. An image being visible and a person being recognised in their desires and life are different conditions. Viewing choices, laughter and bodily gestures are not evidence of an individual’s sexual orientation.

The exhibition adds another relation: the viewer can also be seen watching. Desire’s public appearance becomes a question, without establishing that every visitor feels shame, repression or liberation.

### 06　Visible participation and the ability to participate

A comment can be stored, replayed and exhibited. Its continued appearance does not establish that a visitor can now write a response on the same surface. Several forms of participation need to be distinguished.

The documentation shows text within the displayed images but does not establish whether the original installation supported live comment input, independent playback control or other response mechanisms. It therefore supports neither a confirmed claim of real-time interaction nor a blanket claim that no response was possible.

Watching together is a beginning. Changing the conditions of watching together is a further question.

### 07　“Your”: what is yours to decide?

One way to read the title is through the shift from “You” to “Your”: an address becomes a claim of belonging. CRT televisions and physical tubing also give “Tube” a material resonance. This is an interpretation of the title and installation, not a claim to the artist’s sole original intention.

Does being given a seat include the right to arrange it? Does choosing a screen include deciding which images receive a screen? Does recognising a comment include the ability to change the reading it establishes?

These questions include the artist. Content, equipment and seating have been selected. The displayed images may carry distinct contributions from performers, original production teams, remixers and commenters. An artistic signature should not erase those contributions.

Online circulation is also shaped by regulation, platform rules, commercial distribution and community habits. Multiple screens, low resolution and exposed wiring do not independently prove escape from these arrangements. The work’s critical potential lies in opening their conditions to discussion.

YourTube constructs a position for the viewer and leaves open whether that position can genuinely belong to them.

### Key terms

Danmaku

Comments appearing over video at particular playback positions. They can originate in viewings at different times.

Phonetic reinterpretation / kong’er

Hearing or transcribing sounds as similar-sounding words, producing a new meaning.

The translation of desire

Editing, renaming, jokes and comments reorganise bodies and feelings into forms that can circulate and be discussed.

Co-presence across different times

Responses from different moments appear together in a new playback, without having been posted simultaneously.

The prior socialisation of viewing

Earlier evaluations, cues and reactions are already shaping the starting conditions of a new viewing.

The ability to respond

Being able to offer an answer and potentially affect a relationship, beyond seeing someone else’s expression.

## Installation instructions and playback

The artist’s retained installation instructions add an account of how YourTube was operated: after connecting the main power, individual screens were switched on, and remotes and media players were used to select MP4 files from storage cards. The completed view in the document matches the portfolio installation bearing the label “Fa He / YourTube / 2018,” with the same configuration of six CRT televisions, five flat screens, coloured rods and six connected stools.

The instructions are retained as operating documentation for YourTube. A writing date absent from the document is not inferred, and the number of screens is not equated with the number of independent video channels.

CLOSE READING / 2026 CURATORIAL INTERPRETATION

## YourTube

Do shared seats and visible comments already make a “we”?

### Begin with what happens in the work

Installation records show six CRT televisions, five flat screens and six stools joined with tubing.

The imagery bears traces of re-editing, text overlays and danmaku comment culture.

One structure directs attention toward screens; another joins seats into a ring. Their centres differ. I can sit beside you while everyone watches a different screen, or share an image without knowing how my neighbour understands it. Exposed cables make equipment connections visible, and tubing physically joins the furniture. Those material connections cannot testify on behalf of human relations. The installation gives form to the distance between being connected and understanding one another.

On-screen comments bring previous acts of watching into the present. The bodily imagery has already passed through jokes, phonetic play and shared codes. Familiarity may invite someone in while limiting which kinds of appreciation can be expressed seriously. The work should therefore not simply be praised as interactive. Seeing an earlier comment differs from being able to answer it now; selecting a screen differs from deciding which material can appear. The title’s “your” leaves a concrete question: how much of this position can you actually change?

### The limits of this reading

Eleven devices do not establish eleven independent channels. Live commenting, synchronization and the complete source list remain unverified; viewers’ sexual orientation is not inferred.
