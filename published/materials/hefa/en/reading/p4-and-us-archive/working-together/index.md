# From Rehearsal Actions to Project Agreements

Paper, bodies, materials and onlookers work together; the Personal Chronicle text then proposes how projects begin, proceed and end.

## Reading paper and performing a movement

In a Feedback rehearsal recording, paper is held or spread on mats. Samples show a participant in white moving from hands supported on the mat and head lowered to kneeling with palms together overhead, then extending forward, lowering and raising the head. The camera also moves between the mat and people seated with paper. Denser still-image sampling between source times 00:10:06.005 and 00:10:34.000 makes repeated poses discernible; a separate moment shows a nearby participant in a similar pose.

This is more than seated reading: text and bodily action occupy different positions. Reliable sound evidence is still missing for who demonstrates, who follows a suggestion and which words correspond to the actions. A form of shared work is visible, but the solution to a rehearsal problem has not been reconstructed.

## Shared work takes different material forms

Lisa workshop photographs juxtapose two people standing face to face by a wall with a group exchanging around paper and small materials. Bodily and material tasks may coexist, but neighbouring photographs are not a complete teaching sequence. In Out of Everyday Life, paper lies among seated participants; someone points to it and someone brings a pen to its surface. It becomes an object of shared attention, though its content and any resulting decision remain unverified.

## Outward Monologue: materials change the viewing position

In another scene, two people occupy different sides of a curtain. First a hand holds thread; later a fine line crosses the image near a mouth. Hands work near the fabric, and the camera must move to the other side to see the other person’s action. Denser still-image samples between source times 00:09:40.013 and 00:10:08.007 preserve this local change.

Connection by thread and concealment by fabric make watching more than observing a complete body from a distance. Information appears from different positions. This is a formal reading of visible images, distinct from Feedback’s repeated movements; neither the exercise rules nor participants’ real-life relationships follow from it.

## What would allow more people to initiate projects?

A public text addresses organisation more explicitly: the Personal Chronicle recruitment announcement of 23 March 2023. It proposes starting with a two-to-five-hour one-to-one interview, establishing a record, then having the protagonist initiate a project. The process is to be recorded, and a public presentation marks its end. One initiator cannot run several projects simultaneously. The point is to make beginnings, progress and endings identifiable, rather than prescribe a particular art form.

The text acknowledges that actual events will diverge from expectations. Its first response is procedural: initiators take responsibility for their expression and participants; P4 offers space, documentation, publicity, organisation and planning; the next project follows completion of the previous one. It also uses the beginning and end of recording to distinguish shared creation from everyday life. These are proposed public agreements, not evidence that every earlier or later activity followed them.

## How do an organiser’s and an initiator’s requirements meet?

Personal Chronicle centres the initiator’s expression while requiring a public presentation and process documentation. The organisational question is how a personal wish becomes something others can enter, understand their responsibilities within, and see develop. Boundaries, roles and reporting are the text’s proposed response. We can analyse that design without inventing a dispute it successfully resolved.

Long Long Ago Group offers another proposal: people in different regions take turns hosting their activities while attending others’, and jointly find alternatives to unsuitable venues. Hosting rotates there; Personal Chronicle instead defines projects through a personal record, initiation and presentation. These designs can be compared, but neither implementation nor a direct evolution from one to the other is established.

## How does process become work—and whose work?

In Feedback and Outward Monologue, bodily execution, material connections and what spectators can see already shape the form of the work. Personal Chronicle explicitly places documentation and organisation within a project’s procedure. Together they shift attention from what was finally presented to how working together made presentation possible. But P4 as venue, supporter or custodian is not identical to He Fa as author. His individual creative role in the two recordings has not been separately established, and inclusion here does not make them his solo works.
